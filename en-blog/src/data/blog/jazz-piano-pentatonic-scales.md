<p><strong>Pentatonic scales</strong> are the most efficient improvisational tool on jazz piano — five notes that always sound consonant, easy to finger across the keyboard, and applicable over major, minor, and dominant chords without exhaustive mode theory. McCoy Tyner, Herbie Hancock, and modern players use pentatonics to build lines that glide over complex changes.</p>

<p>This guide covers major, minor, and suspended pentatonics, chord-scale pairings, and piano-specific fingering strategies.</p>

<h2 id="three-essential-pentatonics">Three Essential Pentatonics</h2>

<h3>Major pentatonic</h3>

<p>From C: <strong>C–D–E–G–A</strong> (1, 2, 3, 5, 6). No semitones — why it sounds open and bright.</p>

<h3>Minor pentatonic</h3>

<p>From A: <strong>A–C–D–E–G</strong> (1, b3, 4, 5, b7). Relative of C major pentatonic — same notes, different tonic.</p>

<h3>Suspended (sus) pentatonic</h3>

<p>From C: <strong>C–D–F–G–B</strong> or variations omitting the third — useful on sus4 and modal chords.</p>

<h2 id="why-pentatonics-work-in-jazz">Why Pentatonics Work in Jazz</h2>

<p>Pentatonics avoid guide-tone clashes. Over Cmaj7, C major pentatonic contains chord tones C, E, G plus extensions D (9) and A (13) — all consonant.</p>

<p>Over Dm7, D minor pentatonic (D–F–G–A–C) hits root, b3, 11, 5, b7 — complete minor seventh color without avoid notes.</p>

<p>Over G7, G minor pentatonic (G–Bb–C–D–F) outlines 1, b7, b9/#9, 11, b13 — bluesy dominant without spelling every alteration explicitly.</p>

<h2 id="chord-scale-pairings-quick-reference">Chord-Scale Pairings Quick Reference</h2>

<table>
  <thead>
    <tr><th>Chord</th><th>Pentatonic choice</th><th>Notes (from root)</th></tr>
  </thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>Cmaj7</td><td>C major pentatonic</td><td>C–D–E–G–A</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Dm7</td><td>D minor pentatonic</td><td>D–F–G–A–C</td></tr>
    <tr><td>G7</td><td>G minor or D minor pentatonic</td><td>G–Bb–C–D–F or D–F–G–A–C</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Am7</td><td>A minor pentatonic</td><td>A–C–D–E–G</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>

<p>On a ii–V–I in C (Dm7–G7–Cmaj7), many players use a single D minor pentatonic across all three chords — the "play minor pentatonic from the ii" trick.</p>

<h2 id="pentatonics-on-ii-v-i">Pentatonics on ii–V–I</h2>

<p>Approach 1 — one scale: D minor pentatonic over Dm7, G7, and Cmaj7. Simple, cohesive, slightly bluesy on the tonic.</p>

<p>Approach 2 — shift tonic: D minor on Dm7, G minor on G7, C major on Cmaj7. Clearer harmonic outline.</p>

<p>Approach 3 — pentatonic superimposition: on G7, play C major pentatonic (C–D–E–G–A) for Lydian dominant color (#11 from G's perspective).</p>

<p>Functional context: <a href="https://en.jazzify.jp/blog/jazz-ii-v-i-progression/">ii–V–I progression guide</a>.</p>

<h2 id="modal-application-so-what-and-dorian">Modal Application: So What and Dorian</h2>

<p>Over D Dorian, D minor pentatonic (D–F–G–A–C) is the default solo pool. Over Eb Dorian, Eb minor pentatonic (Eb–Gb–Ab–Bb–Db).</p>

<p>McCoy Tyner-style: play F major pentatonic (F–G–A–C–D) over Dm7 — pentatonic built on the minor 3rd — for stacked fourth implications.</p>

<p>Modal comping context: <a href="https://en.jazzify.jp/blog/jazz-piano-so-what-modal/">So What in D Dorian</a>.</p>

<h2 id="piano-fingering-and-patterns">Piano Fingering and Patterns</h2>

<p>Group pentatonic in two-note-per-finger clusters for speed:</p>

<ul>
  <li>C major pentatonic right hand: 1-2 on C–D, 1-2 on E–G, thumb on A — adjust for hand size</li>
  <li>Run ascending groups of four sixteenth notes: C–D–E–G, D–E–G–A, E–G–A–C</li>
</ul>

<p>Left hand comping shells while right hand pentatonic patterns — standard independence drill.</p>

<h2 id="building-lines-that-do-not-sound-elementary">Building Lines That Do Not Sound Elementary</h2>

<p>Pentatonics sound like exercises when played scale-up scale-down. Fix:</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Skip steps:</strong> C–E–G–A–D–G instead of straight runs</li>
  <li><strong>Rhythm displacement:</strong> Start pattern on beat 2 or and-of-4</li>
  <li><strong>Mix with chord tones:</strong> Insert B (maj7) on Cmaj7 for one beat, return to pentatonic</li>
  <li><strong>Sequences:</strong> Transpose a four-note motif up a whole step each repetition</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="pentatonics-and-quartal-voicings">Pentatonics and Quartal Voicings</h2>

<p>Pentatonic notes align with quartal stacks. C major pentatonic notes C–D–E–G–A fit within quartal voicings built on D or G. When comping quartal in left hand, solo with related pentatonic in right — unified sound.</p>

<p>See <a href="https://en.jazzify.jp/blog/jazz-piano-quartal-voicings/">quartal voicings for jazz piano</a>.</p>

<h2 id="transposing-pentatonics-through-keys">Transposing Pentatonics Through Keys</h2>

<p>Practice D minor pentatonic through the circle of fourths: D, G, C, F, Bb, Eb, Ab, Db, Gb, B, E, A. One key per chorus on a looping ii–V–I backing track.</p>

<p>Say the five notes aloud before playing — connects intellect to fingers.</p>

<h2 id="common-mistakes">Common Mistakes</h2>

<ul>
  <li>Using major pentatonic from the wrong root over minor chords (C major pentatonic on Dm7 includes E natural — clashes with F minor third context when emphasized on strong beats)</li>
  <li>Ignoring rhythm — fast pentatonic runs without swing feel</li>
  <li>Never leaving the pentatonic — add chromatic passing tones from bebop scales occasionally</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="practice-routine">Practice Routine</h2>

<ol>
  <li>Learn C, A, and D minor pentatonics two octaves, hands separate.</li>
  <li>Solo on Dm7–G7–Cmaj7 with D minor pentatonic only — 5 minutes.</li>
  <li>Shift to G minor on G7, C major on Cmaj7 — compare.</li>
  <li>Apply on modal Dm7 vamp — 120 BPM, 8 bars.</li>
  <li>Compose four four-bar pentatonic motifs; transpose to F and Bb.</li>
</ol>

<h2 id="pentatonic-patterns-across-the-keyboard">Pentatonic Patterns Across the Keyboard</h2>

<p>Map five-finger pentatonic positions starting on each chord tone of Dm7: from D, from F, from A, from C. Each rotation highlights different extensions while staying inside minor pentatonic pitch set. Spend one practice session per week rotating starting notes every two bars over a Dm7 vamp — builds fretboard-style fluency on piano.</p>

<p>At faster tempos, shrink patterns to three-note cells (D–F–A, F–A–C) for bebop-style sequences without running full octaves.</p>

<h2 id="major-pentatonic-from-the-fifth">Major Pentatonic From the Fifth</h2>

<p>Over Cmaj7, G major pentatonic (G–A–B–D–E) highlights the 5, 13, 7, 9, and 3 of the chord — useful for bright solo lines on tonic bars. Over G7, D minor pentatonic targets similar extensions relative to the dominant. Map pentatonics from chord tones other than root to escape always starting scales from the same note.</p>

<p>Combine with left-hand shell comping: solo two choruses on a blues in F using only F minor pentatonic, then only Bb minor pentatonic over Bb7 bars.</p>

<h2 id="articulation-and-swing-on-pentatonic-lines">Articulation and Swing on Pentatonic Lines</h2>

<p>Pentatonic runs without articulation sound like exercises. Accent beats 2 and 4; leave short rests between four-note cells; use legato only within cells, not across entire octaves. Record 30 seconds and compare to a Grant Green or McCoy Tyner recording — match articulation density before adding more notes.</p>

<h2 id="group-practice-and-jam-sessions">Group Practice and Jam Sessions</h2>

<p>At jams, pentatonics keep you inside when the form moves faster than you can analyze. Default to minor pentatonic from the ii root over any ii–V–I until chord-scale theory catches up — better than freezing. Upgrade to bebop scales on V bars once the form is stable.</p>

<p>Print a one-page pentatonic reference for the keys you jam in most often — C, F, B-flat, and G — and tape it inside your music stand lid for the first six months of solo study. Update the list as your repertoire expands into new singer keys.</p>

<h2 id="summary">Summary</h2>

<ul>
  <li>Major pentatonic (1-2-3-5-6) suits maj7 and Lydian colors.</li>
  <li>Minor pentatonic (1-b3-4-5-b7) suits m7, Dorian, and dominant blues.</li>
  <li>D minor pentatonic works across entire ii–V–I in C.</li>
  <li>Break scalar runs with skips, rhythm displacement, and motifs.</li>
  <li>Pair with quartal voicings for modern modal soloing.</li>
</ul>
