<p><strong>Walking bass lines</strong> on jazz piano fill the harmonic and rhythmic foundation when no bassist is present — or when you want left-hand independence in trio-style comping. Quarter notes on beats 1–4 outline chord changes, connect roots by step or fifth, and drive swing at medium and up tempos.</p>

<p>This guide covers basic walking principles, note choice on ii–V–I progressions, blues bass lines, and coordination with right-hand voicings.</p>

<h2 id="what-makes-a-bass-line-walk">What Makes a Bass Line "Walk"</h2>

<p>Walking means continuous quarter notes — four per 4/4 bar — with notes chosen to define harmony and forward motion. Unlike root-only patterns (half notes on beat 1), walking fills the bar and implies chord changes before they arrive.</p>

<p>Register: typically C2–C3 for acoustic bass feel on piano. Keep right hand above C4 to avoid mud.</p>

<h2 id="chord-tone-priority">Chord Tone Priority</h2>

<p>On each beat, prefer in order:</p>

<ol>
  <li>Root of current chord</li>
  <li>Fifth or third of current chord</li>
  <li>Root of approaching chord ( anticipation on beat 4)</li>
  <li>Chromatic passing tone between chord tones</li>
</ol>

<p>On Cmaj7 for one bar, a simple walk: C2 (root) – E2 (3rd) – G2 (5th) – A2 (6th or step toward next chord).</p>

<h2 id="walking-through-ii-v-i-in-c">Walking Through ii–V–I in C</h2>

<p>Progression: Dm7 | G7 | Cmaj7 | Cmaj7 (one bar each)</p>

<p>Sample bass line (note names, quarter notes):</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Bar 1 (Dm7):</strong> D – F – A – C</li>
  <li><strong>Bar 2 (G7):</strong> B – D – F – E (E approaches D or leads to Cmaj7)</li>
  <li><strong>Bar 3 (Cmaj7):</strong> C – E – G – B</li>
  <li><strong>Bar 4 (Cmaj7):</strong> A – D – G – C (turnaround setup)</li>
</ul>

<p>Bar 2 beat 1 on B emphasizes G7's third — strong harmonic definition. Chromatic E on beat 4 approaches D or C depending on next bar spelling.</p>

<p>Functional harmony review: <a href="https://en.jazzify.jp/blog/jazz-ii-v-i-progression/">ii–V–I progression</a>.</p>

<h2 id="approach-tones-and-chromaticism">Approach Tones and Chromaticism</h2>

<p>Approach the root of the next chord from a half step above or below:</p>

<ul>
  <li>Approaching Cmaj7 root C: play B on beat 4 of previous bar (below) or Db (above, less common in major)</li>
  <li>Approaching G7 root G: play Ab or F# on beat 4</li>
</ul>

<p>Chromatic passing fills gaps between chord tones: D to F on Dm7 bar can walk D – Eb – E – F if rhythm needs fill — use passing tones on weak beats (2 and 4) when possible.</p>

<h2 id="12-bar-blues-in-f-sample-bass-line">12-Bar Blues in F: Sample Bass Line</h2>

<p>Standard blues changes: F7 | Bb7 | F7 | F7 | Bb7 | Bb7 | F7 | D7 | Gm7 | C7 | F7 | C7 (last bar turnaround varies)</p>

<p>Simplified first four bars (quarter notes):</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>F7:</strong> F – A – C – Eb</li>
  <li><strong>Bb7:</strong> Bb – D – F – Ab</li>
  <li><strong>F7:</strong> F – Eb – D – C (descending approach)</li>
  <li><strong>F7:</strong> F – A – Ab – G (chromatic toward Gm7)</li>
</ul>

<p>Practice blues walking alone at quarter = 100 before adding right-hand comping.</p>

<h2 id="left-hand-alone-vs-stride">Left Hand Alone vs. Stride</h2>

<p><strong>Walking only:</strong> Left hand quarters; right hand silent or later. Builds bass independence.</p>

<p><strong>Stride hybrid:</strong> Bass note on 1 and 3, chord on 2 and 4 — earlier jazz style, not continuous walk but related skill.</p>

<p><strong>Walk + comp:</strong> Left hand walk entire bar; right hand stabs voicings on 2 and 4 or and-of-2. Hardest coordination — master walk alone first.</p>

<h2 id="coordinating-with-right-hand-voicings">Coordinating with Right-Hand Voicings</h2>

<p>Rules for walk + comp:</p>

<ul>
  <li>Right hand avoids doubling bass note in same octave</li>
  <li>Comp short — do not sustain through every bass note or harmony muddies</li>
  <li>Start with two-handed rhythm: LH quarters, RH one voicing on beat 2 only</li>
  <li>Add beat-4 RH hit when LH walk is automatic</li>
</ul>

<p>Rootless voicings in right hand while left walks roots — classic trio simulation. Voicing guide: <a href="https://en.jazzify.jp/blog/jazz-piano-left-hand-voicings/">jazz piano left-hand voicings</a>.</p>

<h2 id="triton-substitution-in-the-bass">Triton Substitution in the Bass</h2>

<p>On G7 bar walking to Cmaj7, substitute Db7 bass motion:</p>

<p>Db – F – Cb – B (Cb/B resolves to C)</p>

<p>Chromatic bass descent D – Db – C on bars Dm7–G7–Cmaj7 when substituting. See <a href="https://en.jazzify.jp/blog/jazz-piano-tritone-substitution/">tritone substitution</a>.</p>

<h2 id="tempo-and-feel">Tempo and Feel</h2>

<ul>
  <li><strong>60–90 BPM:</strong> Learning zone — say note names aloud while playing</li>
  <li><strong>100–130 BPM:</strong> Performance range for medium swing standards</li>
  <li><strong>130+ BPM:</strong> Simplify lines — roots and fifths dominate; fewer chromatic fills</li>
</ul>

<p>Swing eighths in right hand while left walks quarters — left hand straight quarters, right hand triplet feel. Do not swing the bass line heavily unless stylistically intentional (some Latin-jazz walks excepted).</p>

<h2 id="common-mistakes">Common Mistakes</h2>

<ul>
  <li>Roots only on beat 1 with three beats rest — not walking</li>
  <li>Jumping octaves randomly — keep smooth stepwise motion when possible</li>
  <li>Wrong register — too high (above C3) loses bass character</li>
  <li>Adding right-hand melody before bass is solid — coordination collapse</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="practice-routine">Practice Routine</h2>

<ol>
  <li>Walk ii–V–I in C, F, Bb — 5 min each key, LH only, 80 BPM.</li>
  <li>12-bar blues in F — LH only, 100 BPM, 10 min.</li>
  <li>Add RH shell on beat 2 — same blues, 10 min.</li>
  <li>Walk + rootless comp on one chorus of active standard.</li>
  <li>Transpose blues walk to B-flat and E-flat.</li>
</ol>

<h2 id="two-feel-and-latin-bass-alternatives">Two-Feel and Latin Bass Alternatives</h2>

<p>Not every jazz tune walks quarter notes throughout. Bossa nova often uses roots on 1 and 3 with chord tones on 2 and 4. Samba patterns repeat syncopated cells. Learn walking first, then adapt — the note-choice logic (roots, chord tones, approach tones) still applies; only rhythmic grid changes.</p>

<p>On medium swing standards at 140+ BPM, left-hand walking plus right-hand single-note lines is rare in solo piano — consider stride or root-on-one patterns instead to keep texture manageable.</p>

<h2 id="practice-with-backing-tracks-minus-bass">Practice With Backing Tracks Minus Bass</h2>

<p>Some play-along tracks offer mix-minus-bass stems. Walking over those files tests whether your line defines changes without hiding behind a recorded bassist. If minus-bass tracks are unavailable, mute low frequencies mentally and ask whether another musician could infer the chord progression from your line alone — the standard test for audible walking.</p>

<h2 id="physical-endurance">Physical Endurance</h2>

<p>Walking bass at quarter = 120 for full choruses fatigues the left hand before the right. Build endurance gradually: two choruses daily for one week, then three, then four. Stretch forearms between sets. Tension in the wrist produces uneven quarter notes audible to bassists and drummers.</p>

<h2 id="summary">Summary</h2>

<ul>
  <li>Walking bass uses continuous quarter notes in C2–C3 register.</li>
  <li>Prioritize roots, chord tones, approach tones, chromatic passing.</li>
  <li>Master ii–V–I and blues bass lines before adding right-hand comp.</li>
  <li>Triton subs create chromatic bass descent — D–Db–C.</li>
  <li>Build tempo gradually; simplify lines above 130 BPM.</li>
</ul>
