<h2 id="ear-training-skills-jazz-pianists-need">Ear Training Skills Jazz Pianists Need</h2>

<p>Ordered by priority for beginners through intermediate players:</p>

<ol>
  <li><strong>Interval recognition</strong> — foundation for everything else.</li>
  <li><strong>Chord quality identification</strong> — maj7, m7, dom7, half-dim, dim7 by sound.</li>
  <li><strong>Root movement hearing</strong> — identify bass motion by interval (down a fifth, up a half step).</li>
  <li><strong>Short progression recognition</strong> — hear a ii-V-I and identify the key.</li>
  <li><strong>Guide-tone hearing</strong> — recognize the 3rd and 7th of a chord without the root.</li>
  <li><strong>Melodic phrase call-and-response</strong> — hear a two-bar phrase, play it back.</li>
  <li><strong>Voicing recognition</strong> — distinguish shell voicing from rootless voicing by texture.</li>
</ol>

<p>Most general ear-training apps cover step 1 thoroughly and step 2 partially. Steps 3–7 require jazz-specific tools.</p>

<h2 id="what-to-look-for-in-a-jazz-ear-training-app">What to Look For in a Jazz Ear Training App</h2>

<h3>MIDI keyboard input for responses</h3>

<p>Tapping multiple-choice buttons trains recognition but not execution. The best apps ask you to <em>play</em> what you hear — a Dm7 chord, a four-note phrase, a ii-V-I bass line. MIDI input closes the gap between hearing and playing.</p>

<h3>Chord-quality modules with extensions</h3>

<p>Basic trainers test major vs. minor triads. Jazz apps must include maj7, m7, dom7, m7b5, dim7, and eventually dom7alt and maj7#11. Test with the extensions you encounter in real standards.</p>

<h3>Progression-level exercises</h3>

<p>Hearing isolated chords is step one. Hearing a Dm7–G7–Cmaj7 sequence and identifying the key center is step two. The app should drill progressions, not just individual harmonies.</p>

<h3>Call-and-response phrasing</h3>

<p>A short melodic fragment — two bars, swing feel — plays, then you reproduce it on the keyboard. This builds the transcription skill that every jazz teacher recommends and few apps provide.</p>

<h3>Integration with harmony curriculum</h3>

<p>Ear training should not live in a separate silo. After learning shell voicings in a harmony quest, the ear-training module should test whether you can <em>hear</em> those voicings in context. Jazzify integrates ear training into guided quests so listening and playing reinforce each other.</p>

<h2 id="ear-training-exercises-to-do-daily">Ear Training Exercises to Do Daily</h2>

<h3>Five-minute interval warm-up</h3>

<p>Play an interval (m3, P5, m7) on the keyboard. Sing it back. Check with the app or a tuner. Do 10 intervals in random order.</p>

<h3>Chord quality quiz (10 minutes)</h3>

<p>App plays a chord. You play it back on the keyboard or identify its quality. Start with triads, add seventh chords within two weeks, add extensions within two months.</p>

<h3>Progression dictation (10 minutes)</h3>

<p>App plays a four-bar progression (e.g., Am7–D7–Gmaj7–Cmaj7). Identify the key and each chord by ear. Play the bass line on the keyboard.</p>

<h3>Phrase call-and-response (10 minutes)</h3>

<p>App plays a two-bar melodic fragment using chord tones. Reproduce it on the keyboard. Start slow (60 BPM), increase tempo as accuracy improves.</p>

<h2 id="how-ear-training-connects-to-other-jazz-skills">How Ear Training Connects to Other Jazz Skills</h2>

<p>Ear training is not a separate subject — it supports every other skill:</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Comping</strong> — hearing chord quality tells you which voicing to choose before you look at the chart.</li>
  <li><strong>Improvisation</strong> — hearing guide tones helps you target resolutions in your lines.</li>
  <li><strong>Transcription</strong> — interval and phrase recognition speed up the process of copying recordings.</li>
  <li><strong>Repertoire</strong> — hearing root movement helps you memorize changes without staring at the lead sheet.</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="common-ear-training-mistakes">Common Ear Training Mistakes</h2>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Only doing interval drills</strong> — intervals are foundation, not destination.</li>
  <li><strong>Identifying chords without playing them back</strong> — recognition without execution does not transfer to the bandstand.</li>
  <li><strong>Practicing ear training separately from repertoire</strong> — apply every skill to a tune you are learning.</li>
  <li><strong>Skipping singing</strong> — sing every interval and phrase before playing it; singing connects your ear to your body.</li>
  <li><strong>Using only major key exercises</strong> — jazz lives in minor keys, modal harmony, and secondary dominants.</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="ear-training-without-an-app-low-tech-alternatives">Ear Training Without an App: Low-Tech Alternatives</h2>

<p>Before apps existed, jazz students trained ears with:</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Singing along with recordings</strong> — match pitch with a vocalist or horn player on a standard.</li>
  <li><strong>Playing bass lines by ear</strong> — figure out root movement on a recording before checking the chart.</li>
  <li><strong>Partner dictation</strong> — a teacher or friend plays chords; you name them.</li>
</ul>

<p>These methods still work and cost nothing. Apps add structure, progressive difficulty, and instant verification — but the underlying skill is the same: hear something, identify it, reproduce it.</p>

<h2 id="evaluating-an-ear-training-app-quick-checklist">Evaluating an Ear Training App: Quick Checklist</h2>

<ul>
  <li>Does it test chord qualities beyond major and minor triads?</li>
  <li>Can you respond by playing on a MIDI keyboard, not just tapping buttons?</li>
  <li>Does it include progression-level exercises (ii-V-I, blues, turnarounds)?</li>
  <li>Is ear training integrated with harmony and improvisation curriculum?</li>
  <li>Does it include phrase call-and-response?</li>
  <li>Can you practice in minor keys and with extensions?</li>
</ul>

<p>If three or more answers are no, the app is a general trainer, not a jazz tool.</p>

<h2 id="progression-difficulty-levels-in-ear-training">Progression Difficulty Levels in Ear Training</h2>

<p>Move through these stages in order — skipping ahead produces gaps:</p>

<ol>
  <li>Major vs. minor triads</li>
  <li>maj7 vs. m7 vs. dom7</li>
  <li>m7b5 and dim7</li>
  <li>ii-V-I in one key (slow tempo)</li>
  <li>ii-V-I in random keys</li>
  <li>Turnarounds and backdoor dominants</li>
  <li>Short melodic phrases (2 bars) with chord-tone content</li>
</ol>

<p>Spend at least one week on each stage before advancing. Jazzify's ear-training quests follow a similar progression, adjusting difficulty as your accuracy improves.</p>
