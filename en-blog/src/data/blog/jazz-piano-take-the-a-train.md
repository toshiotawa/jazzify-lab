<p><strong>Take the A Train</strong> — Billy Strayhorn's signature piece for the Duke Ellington orchestra — is essential swing repertoire for jazz piano. Usually in <strong>C major</strong> with a bright 4/4 bounce, it combines straightforward ii–V–I motion with rhythmic drive that rewards crisp comping and economical solo lines.</p>

<p>This guide covers the chord progression, left-hand shells, swing comping patterns, and solo ideas using scales and arpeggios. Melody is not written out; learn the head from a recording or lead sheet separately.</p>

<h2 id="form-and-tempo">Form and Tempo</h2>

<p>Take the A Train uses 32-bar A–A–B–A form. Performance tempo ranges from medium swing (quarter = 120) to bright (160+). Ellington recordings vary; start comping at 100 BPM and increase when voicings stay clean.</p>

<p>The A sections emphasize C major with quick dominant cycles. The B section modulates for contrast before returning home.</p>

<h2 id="a-section-harmony-bars-1-8">A Section Harmony (Bars 1–8)</h2>

<p>A common Real Book progression for the first eight bars in C:</p>

<ul>
  <li>Bar 1: <strong>C6</strong> or <strong>Cmaj7</strong> (C–E–G–A or C–E–G–B)</li>
  <li>Bar 2: <strong>D7</strong> (D–F#–A–C) — often labeled D7 or secondary dominant</li>
  <li>Bar 3: <strong>Dm7</strong> (D–F–A–C)</li>
  <li>Bar 4: <strong>G7</strong> (G–B–D–F)</li>
  <li>Bar 5: <strong>C6</strong> or <strong>Cmaj7</strong></li>
  <li>Bar 6: <strong>C6</strong> / <strong>A7</strong> (two chords) or sustained C with passing A7</li>
  <li>Bar 7: <strong>Dm7</strong></li>
  <li>Bar 8: <strong>G7</strong></li>
</ul>

<p>Bars 3–4 and 7–8 are classic ii–V–I fragments (Dm7–G7–C). Bar 2's D7 often functions as a secondary dominant (V/V or approach to Dm7). Speak Roman numerals while playing roots: I – II7 – iim7 – V7 – I.</p>

<h2 id="second-a-section-bars-9-16">Second A Section (Bars 9–16)</h2>

<p>Typically parallels bars 1–8 with possible substitution in bars 13–16 — some charts insert <strong>E7</strong> (E–G#–B–D) as a passing dominant before Am7 or return to Dm7. Check your edition.</p>

<p>Practice bars 9–16 with identical voicings to bars 1–8 first; add variations only after the form is automatic.</p>

<h2 id="b-section-bars-17-24">B Section (Bars 17–24)</h2>

<p>The bridge often moves through:</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Fmaj7</strong> (F–A–C–E) — IV in C</li>
  <li><strong>F#m7b5</strong> (F#–A–C–E) or <strong>Ab7</strong> — chromatic approach</li>
  <li><strong>Em7 – A7 – Dm7 – G7</strong> — extended ii–V chain back toward C</li>
</ul>

<p>Some arrangements simplify the bridge to a cycle of dominants: <strong>C7 – F7 – Bb7 – Eb7</strong> descending by fourths. Label your chart's version explicitly.</p>

<p>The Em7–A7–Dm7–G7 sequence is worth extracting for all-key practice — each pair is a ii–V toward the next key center.</p>

<h2 id="final-a-and-tag-bars-25-32">Final A and Tag (Bars 25–32)</h2>

<p>Return to A material. Final two bars may use a turnaround:</p>

<p><strong>Am7 – D7 – Dm7 – G7</strong> or <strong>Am7 – Ab7 – G7 – C6</strong></p>

<p>The Ab7 (Ab–Cb–Eb–Gb) tritone substitution for D7 adds Ellington-era color — see <a href="https://en.jazzify.jp/blog/jazz-piano-tritone-substitution/">tritone substitution for jazz piano</a>.</p>

<h2 id="shell-voicings-for-swing-comping">Shell Voicings for Swing Comping</h2>

<ul>
  <li><strong>C6:</strong> C2, E3, A3</li>
  <li><strong>D7:</strong> D2, F#3, C4</li>
  <li><strong>Dm7:</strong> D2, F3, C4</li>
  <li><strong>G7:</strong> G2, B3, F4</li>
  <li><strong>Fmaj7:</strong> F2, A3, E4</li>
  <li><strong>A7:</strong> A1, C#3, G3</li>
</ul>

<p>At medium swing, comp shells on beats 1 and 3 or use Freddie Green–style quarter-note pulses in the left hand while the right hand adds chord stabs.</p>

<h2 id="swing-comping-rhythms">Swing Comping Rhythms</h2>

<p><strong>Charleston pattern</strong> (essential on this tune): beat 1 quarter, and-of-2 eighth, rest on 3–4. Apply through Dm7–G7–C for one chorus.</p>

<p><strong>Two-feel vs. four-feel:</strong> Ellington bands sometimes open with two-feel (half notes) before walking bass four-feel. Practice toggling: bars 1–4 two-feel, bars 5–8 four-feel Charleston.</p>

<p><strong>Anticipations:</strong> Push chord changes to beat 4 of the previous bar — especially G7 anticipating resolution to C on bar 5.</p>

<h2 id="rootless-voicings-in-c">Rootless Voicings in C</h2>

<ul>
  <li><strong>C6/9:</strong> E3, A3, D4, G4</li>
  <li><strong>Dm9 Type B:</strong> C3, F3, A3, E4</li>
  <li><strong>G13 Type A:</strong> B3, E4, F4, A4</li>
  <li><strong>Fmaj9:</strong> E3, A3, C4, G4</li>
</ul>

<p>Keep voicings compact — this tune rewards clarity over dense extensions. Reference <a href="https://en.jazzify.jp/blog/type-a-type-b-rootless-voicings/">Type A and Type B rootless voicings</a>.</p>

<h2 id="solo-ideas-without-the-melody">Solo Ideas Without the Melody</h2>

<p>Use these pitch sources over the A section:</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>C major scale</strong> (C–D–E–F–G–A–B) over C and F chords</li>
  <li><strong>D Mixolydian</strong> (D–E–F#–G–A–B–C) over D7</li>
  <li><strong>G Mixolydian</strong> over G7</li>
  <li><strong>D dorian</strong> (D–E–F–G–A–B–C) over Dm7</li>
</ul>

<p>Bebop approach: run <strong>G bebop dominant</strong> (G–A–B–C–D–E–F–F#) over G7 bars, landing chord tones on beats 1 and 3. Details in <a href="https://en.jazzify.jp/blog/jazz-piano-bebop-scales/">bebop scales for jazz piano</a>.</p>

<p>Arpeggio practice: Dm7 (D–F–A–C) ascending, G7 (G–B–D–F) descending, resolve to C6 chord tones (C–E–G–A).</p>

<h2 id="ensemble-considerations">Ensemble Considerations</h2>

<p>As the only comping instrument in a small group, lock with the bassist's quarter-note pulse. Hit chord anticipations together on and-of-4 before section changes.</p>

<p>In big-band style piano, leave rhythmic holes — do not fill every beat. Strayhorn's arrangement has written kicks; piano comping supports, not overwhelms.</p>

<h2 id="practice-plan">Practice Plan</h2>

<ol>
  <li>Roots and shells, half notes, 80 BPM.</li>
  <li>Charleston comp through A section at 100 BPM.</li>
  <li>Add B section; full 32 bars at 120 BPM.</li>
  <li>Solo two choruses using modes and bebop dominant on V chords.</li>
  <li>Play-along with swing backing track in C.</li>
</ol>

<h2 id="ellington-band-context-for-pianists">Ellington Band Context for Pianists</h2>

<p>In the Ellington orchestra, Strayhorn's arrangement left specific harmonic hits for the sax section. Solo piano can imply those kicks with short staccato voicings on beats 2 and 4 during the A section — not constant filling. Listen to Duke's 1941 recording for macro form; notice how piano enters sparingly behind the trumpet statement of the head.</p>

<p>When comping for a horn player, leave space in the middle register (F4–B4) where the melody lives. Your voicings sit below or above — rarely both — unless arranging a dedicated chord-melody performance.</p>

<h2 id="shout-chorus-and-endings">Shout Chorus and Endings</h2>

<p>Dance-band arrangements sometimes add a shout chorus with augmented chords and rhythmic hits. Solo piano can hint at this energy with accented G13 voicings (G–B–D–F–A–E) on beats 2 and 4 during the final A section — no melody required. End with a two-bar tag: Am7–Ab7–G7–C6, landing on a spread C6/9 (C–E–G–A–D) for a classic Broadway finish.</p>

<h2 id="gig-bag-readiness">Gig Bag Readiness</h2>

<p>Take the A Train appears on short notice at jazz jams because everyone knows the form. Keep a mental checklist: key (usually C), tempo (120–140), ending tag (Am7–Ab7–G7–C), and whether the group plays the bridge twice or once. Call the key aloud before counting off — prevents four musicians starting in different transpositions.</p>

<p>Keep a one-page chord outline in your gig folder next to the Real Book for last-minute refreshers.</p>

<h2 id="summary">Summary</h2>

<ul>
  <li>Take the A Train is 32-bar A–A–B–A swing in C major.</li>
  <li>A sections feature D7, Dm7–G7–C, and repeated ii–V motion.</li>
  <li>Bridge modulates through F and ii–V chains back to C.</li>
  <li>Charleston rhythm and anticipations define authentic comping feel.</li>
  <li>Solo using Mixolydian dominants, dorian on ii, and bebop scales on V7.</li>
</ul>
