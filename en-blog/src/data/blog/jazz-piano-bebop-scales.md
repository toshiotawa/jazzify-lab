<p><strong>Bebop scales</strong> add one chromatic passing tone to seven-note modes so chord tones land on strong beats when you run eighth-note lines. Charlie Parker and Bud Powell did not call them "bebop scales," but the concept explains why their scalular lines align so cleanly with harmony on fast tempos.</p>

<p>This guide covers major, dominant, and minor bebop scales, piano fingering, and line-building on ii–V–I progressions.</p>

<h2 id="the-core-idea-chord-tones-on-downbeats">The Core Idea: Chord Tones on Downbeats</h2>

<p>In 4/4 swing, eighth-note lines have two notes per beat. Diatonic seven-note scales produce chord tones on offbeats when run straight up or down. Adding a chromatic passing tone creates an eight-note scale — chord tones sync with beats 1, 2, 3, 4 when you start on the root ascending.</p>

<p>Example: ascending G Mixolydian with added major 7th (F#) as passing tone between G and A — G, A, B, C, D, E, F, F# — adjusted so chord tones G, B, D, F fall on strong beats.</p>

<h2 id="dominant-bebop-scale-mixolydian-natural-7">Dominant Bebop Scale (Mixolydian + natural 7)</h2>

<p>On G7, the dominant bebop scale is:</p>

<p><strong>G – A – B – C – D – E – F – F#</strong> (G Mixolydian plus major 7th F# as chromatic passing tone between F and G in descending form, or placed to align downbeats in ascending form)</p>

<p>Standard ascending from G for strong-beat chord tones:</p>

<p><strong>G – A – B – C – D – E – F – F# – G</strong></p>

<p>Chord tones G, B, D, F (root, 3, 5, b7) hit downbeats when the scale starts on G on beat 1.</p>

<p>Practice ascending and descending in the right hand at quarter = 100, swing eighths.</p>

<h2 id="major-bebop-scale-ionian-b6">Major Bebop Scale (Ionian + b6)</h2>

<p>On Cmaj7, major bebop adds Ab (b6) as passing tone:</p>

<p><strong>C – D – E – F – G – A – Bb – B – C</strong></p>

<p>Chord tones C, E, G, B land on downbeats ascending from C on beat 1. The Bb passing tone adds brief chromatic color without leaving the major sound.</p>

<h2 id="minor-bebop-scale-dorian-major-7">Minor Bebop Scale (Dorian + major 7)</h2>

<p>On Dm7, D Dorian plus C# (major 7th) as passing tone:</p>

<p><strong>D – E – F – G – A – B – C – C# – D</strong></p>

<p>Creates bebop alignment on minor chords in ii–V–I contexts. The C# resolves toward D or functions as leading tone toward D if thinking temporarily in D minor harmonic.</p>

<h2 id="applying-scales-on-ii-v-i-in-c">Applying Scales on ii–V–I in C</h2>

<p>Progression: Dm7–G7–Cmaj7</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Dm7:</strong> D minor bebop (D–E–F–G–A–B–C–C#–D)</li>
  <li><strong>G7:</strong> G dominant bebop (G–A–B–C–D–E–F–F#–G)</li>
  <li><strong>Cmaj7:</strong> C major bebop (C–D–E–F–G–A–Bb–B–C)</li>
</ul>

<p>Connect scales without stopping at bar lines — the hallmark of bebop fluency. End G7 bar on F or B that resolves to C or E on Cmaj7 downbeat.</p>

<p>Harmonic foundation: <a href="https://en.jazzify.jp/blog/jazz-ii-v-i-progression/">ii–V–I progression</a>.</p>

<h2 id="enclosure-and-approach-notes">Enclosure and Approach Notes</h2>

<p>Bebop scales are one tool; enclosure surrounds target notes with chromatic neighbors. Target E on Cmaj7: play F–Eb–E or D#–F–E before landing.</p>

<p>Combine scale segments with enclosures on beat 4 of each bar for authentic Parker-era language.</p>

<h2 id="right-hand-line-construction">Right-Hand Line Construction</h2>

<p>Four-bar model on ii–V–I:</p>

<ol>
  <li>Bar 1 (Dm7): Ascend D minor bebop from D3 to D4</li>
  <li>Bar 2 (G7): Descend G dominant bebop from D4 to G3</li>
  <li>Bar 3 (Cmaj7): Ascend C major bebop from E4 to B4</li>
  <li>Bar 4: Rest or rhythmic pickup into next chorus</li>
</ol>

<p>Vary starting notes — begin bar 2 on B (3rd of G7) instead of G.</p>

<h2 id="left-hand-comping-while-soloing">Left Hand Comping While Soloing</h2>

<p>Keep left hand minimal: shell voicings or rootless hits on beats 1 and 3. Full bebop scale practice in right hand alone first; add left hand when line is automatic.</p>

<p>Left-hand voicing reference: <a href="https://en.jazzify.jp/blog/jazz-piano-left-hand-voicings/">jazz piano left-hand voicings</a>.</p>

<h2 id="bebop-vs-pentatonic">Bebop vs. Pentatonic</h2>

<p>Pentatonics simplify soloing; bebop scales align with functional harmony at fast tempos. Use pentatonics for modal vamps; bebop scales for changes-heavy standards.</p>

<p>Compare: <a href="https://en.jazzify.jp/blog/jazz-piano-pentatonic-scales/">pentatonic scales for jazz piano</a>.</p>

<h2 id="common-errors">Common Errors</h2>

<ul>
  <li>Running scales without swing articulation — accent beats 2 and 4 subtly</li>
  <li>Starting bebop scale on wrong beat — chord tones miss downbeats</li>
  <li>Using dominant bebop on minor chords without adjustment — stick to minor bebop on ii</li>
  <li>Only ascending — descending lines are equally essential</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="transposition-drill">Transposition Drill</h2>

<p>Play ii–V–I in F: Gm7–C7–Fmaj7 with G minor, C dominant, and F major bebop scales. Continue around circle of fourths — one key per two minutes at 120 BPM.</p>

<h2 id="connecting-to-diminished-and-altered-sounds">Connecting to Diminished and Altered Sounds</h2>

<p>Bebop dominant scale includes natural 7 (F# on G7) — one chromatic step from the root. For more tension, pair with diminished arpeggio fragments on beats 3–4 of G7 bars.</p>

<p>See <a href="https://en.jazzify.jp/blog/jazz-piano-diminished-chords/">diminished chords for jazz piano</a>.</p>

<h2 id="practice-routine">Practice Routine</h2>

<ol>
  <li>Memorize G dominant bebop two octaves, hands separate.</li>
  <li>Ascend/descend with metronome on 2 and 4, quarter = 80.</li>
  <li>Four-bar ii–V–I line in C; write three variations.</li>
  <li>Transpose to Bb and G at quarter = 100.</li>
  <li>Solo one chorus on "Take the A Train" changes using bebop scales primarily.</li>
</ol>

<h2 id="hearing-bebop-scale-in-transcription">Hearing Bebop Scale in Transcription</h2>

<p>When transcribing Parker or Powell lines, mark where chromatic passing tones appear relative to bar lines. You will notice many segments match bebop dominant or major bebop scales for two beats at a time, then break with enclosure or arpeggio. Use scales as scaffolding — not as the complete solo language. Transcription reveals rhythm and accent placement that scales alone cannot supply.</p>

<h2 id="descending-bebop-lines">Descending Bebop Lines</h2>

<p>Ascending bebop scales align chord tones on downbeats; descending requires equal attention. From G7, descend G dominant bebop starting on beat 1 with G on the downbeat, ensuring B, D, F, and G still land strongly on beats as you pass through the scale. Write one descending four-bar line per week in a notebook — rhythm notation matters as much as pitch choice.</p>

<h2 id="metronome-placement-for-scale-practice">Metronome Placement for Scale Practice</h2>

<p>Place click on beats 2 and 4 while running bebop scales in swing eighths — internalizes jazz pulse. When scales rush, move click to all four beats temporarily, then revert to 2-and-4 only after three clean passes at the target tempo.</p>

<h2 id="summary">Summary</h2>

<ul>
  <li>Bebop scales add one chromatic passing tone so chord tones hit downbeats in eighth-note lines.</li>
  <li>Dominant bebop = Mixolydian + natural 7; major bebop = Ionian + b6; minor bebop = Dorian + major 7.</li>
  <li>Apply D minor, G dominant, and C major bebop on ii–V–I in C.</li>
  <li>Combine with enclosures and swing articulation — not straight scale runs.</li>
  <li>Transpose through all keys on a metronome.</li>
</ul>
