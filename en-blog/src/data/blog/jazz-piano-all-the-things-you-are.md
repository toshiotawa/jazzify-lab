<p><strong>All the Things You Are</strong> is a masterclass in modulation for jazz piano. Jerome Kern's harmony wanders through several key centers — Ab major, C major, Eb major, and more — connected by elegant ii–V–I progressions. Pianists study it to learn long-range voice leading, not just local chord grips.</p>

<p>This guide maps the harmonic journey, provides voicing examples in Ab, and explains how to connect chords by guide tones. No melody notation is included; pair this with a lead sheet for the tune itself.</p>

<h2 id="why-this-tune-matters-for-pianists">Why This Tune Matters for Pianists</h2>

<p>Unlike blues-based standards, All the Things You Are moves through extended tonicizations. Each eight-bar section establishes a temporary tonic before pivoting. If you can comp through it smoothly, you can handle most Broadway-era jazz repertoire.</p>

<p>Most Real Book editions place the tune in <strong>Ab major</strong>. The opening phrase already implies F minor before confirming Ab major — typical Kern ambiguity.</p>

<h2 id="opening-section-f-minor-to-ab-major">Opening Section: F Minor to Ab Major</h2>

<p>First eight bars (common Real Book outline):</p>

<ul>
  <li>Bar 1: <strong>Fm7</strong> (F–Ab–C–Eb)</li>
  <li>Bar 2: <strong>Bbm7</strong> (Bb–Db–F–Ab)</li>
  <li>Bar 3: <strong>Eb7</strong> (Eb–G–Bb–Db)</li>
  <li>Bar 4: <strong>Abmaj7</strong> (Ab–C–Eb–G)</li>
  <li>Bar 5: <strong>Dbmaj7</strong> (Db–F–Ab–C)</li>
  <li>Bar 6: <strong>Gbmaj7</strong> (Gb–Bb–Db–F) or <strong>Db6</strong> — edition varies</li>
  <li>Bar 7: <strong>Dbmaj7</strong></li>
  <li>Bar 8: <strong>Dm7</strong> (D–F–A–C) — pivot toward C major</li>
</ul>

<p>Bars 1–4 are a ii–V–I in Ab: Fm7–Bbm7–Eb7–Abmaj7. Label it explicitly when studying. Bars 5–7 stay in Db major territory before the Dm7 pivot surprises the ear toward C.</p>

<h2 id="second-section-c-major-center">Second Section: C Major Center</h2>

<p>Bars 9–16 often proceed:</p>

<ul>
  <li>Bar 9: <strong>G7</strong> (G–B–D–F) — V of C</li>
  <li>Bar 10: <strong>Cmaj7</strong> (C–E–G–B)</li>
  <li>Bar 11: <strong>Am7</strong> (A–C–E–G)</li>
  <li>Bar 12: <strong>D7</strong> (D–F#–A–C)</li>
  <li>Bar 13: <strong>Gmaj7</strong> (G–B–D–F#)</li>
  <li>Bar 14: <strong>E7</strong> (E–G#–B–D)</li>
  <li>Bar 15: <strong>Am7</strong></li>
  <li>Bar 16: <strong>Abm7</strong> (Ab–Cb–Eb–Gb) or <strong>Fm7</strong> — setup for bridge</li>
</ul>

<p>Bars 11–13 form ii–V–I in G major. Bar 14's E7 is a V7/vi in G or secondary dominant toward Am. Track guide tones: C# on D7 resolves to B on Gmaj7; G# on E7 resolves to A on Am7.</p>

<h2 id="bridge-eb-major-and-return">Bridge: Eb Major and Return</h2>

<p>Bars 17–24 commonly feature:</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Cm7 – Fm7 – Bb7 – Ebmaj7</strong> (ii–V–I in Eb)</li>
  <li>Then <strong>Abmaj7</strong> or chromatic approach chords back toward Ab</li>
  <li>Late bridge: <strong>Db7</strong> → <strong>Gbmaj7</strong> or similar dominant cycle motion</li>
</ul>

<p>The bridge's ii–V–I in Eb (Cm7–Fm7–Bb7–Ebmaj7) is a standalone drill worth transposing to all keys. See <a href="https://en.jazzify.jp/blog/jazz-ii-v-i-progression/">ii–V–I progression practice</a>.</p>

<h2 id="final-section-and-cadence">Final Section and Cadence</h2>

<p>Bars 25–32 return toward Ab with familiar ii–V–I language:</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Fm7 – Bbm7 – Eb7 – Abmaj7</strong> restates the opening cadence</li>
  <li>Some charts add a coda with <strong>Dbmaj7 – Eb7 – Abmaj7</strong></li>
</ul>

<p>End voicings on Abmaj7 with the ninth (Bb) for a open, resolved color: Eb3, Ab3, C4, Bb4.</p>

<h2 id="guide-tone-voice-leading">Guide Tone Voice Leading</h2>

<p>Connect thirds and sevenths across changes. Example from Fm7 to Bbm7 to Eb7:</p>

<ul>
  <li>Fm7: Eb (7th) and Ab (3rd) in left hand</li>
  <li>Bbm7: Ab (7th) and Db (3rd) — Ab common tone</li>
  <li>Eb7: Db (7th) and G (3rd) — Db common tone</li>
  <li>Abmaj7: G (7th) and C (3rd) — G common tone</li>
</ul>

<p>Practice this line slowly without roots. Add roots back once the upper voice motion is automatic. More on guide tones: <a href="https://en.jazzify.jp/blog/jazz-piano-guide-tones/">jazz piano guide tones</a>.</p>

<h2 id="rootless-voicings-in-ab">Rootless Voicings in Ab</h2>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Fm7 Type B:</strong> Eb3, Ab3, C4, F4</li>
  <li><strong>Bbm7 Type A:</strong> Db3, F3, Ab3, C4</li>
  <li><strong>Eb7 Type B:</strong> Db3, G3, Bb3, F4</li>
  <li><strong>Abmaj7 Type A:</strong> C3, G3, Bb3, Eb4</li>
  <li><strong>Cmaj7 Type A:</strong> E3, B3, D4, G4</li>
  <li><strong>D7 Type A:</strong> F#3, C4, Eb4, A4</li>
</ul>

<p>Alternate Type A and Type B as roots move by fourth or fifth to minimize hand jumps.</p>

<h2 id="modulation-map-for-study">Modulation Map for Study</h2>

<p>Draw a roadmap before comping full form:</p>

<ol>
  <li>Ab major (bars 1–4, return later)</li>
  <li>Db major (bars 5–7)</li>
  <li>C major (bars 9–10, 11–12)</li>
  <li>G major (bars 11–13)</li>
  <li>Eb major (bridge)</li>
</ol>

<p>When you lose place in a chorus, identify which ii–V–I you are inside — recovery is faster than counting bars blindly.</p>

<h2 id="comping-considerations">Comping Considerations</h2>

<p>Ballad-to-medium tempo (quarter = 72–120). Use:</p>

<ul>
  <li>Half-note and whole-note comping in ballad sections</li>
  <li>Light Charleston in medium swing sections</li>
  <li>Long pedal on common tones during modulations</li>
</ul>

<p>Avoid competing with a vocalist through dense reharmonization until the basic form is solid.</p>

<h2 id="practice-sequence">Practice Sequence</h2>

<ol>
  <li>Play roots only through 32 bars; speak key centers aloud.</li>
  <li>Add shell voicings; isolate Fm7–Bbm7–Eb7–Abmaj7 in all keys.</li>
  <li>Run guide-tone lines through section transitions.</li>
  <li>Full rootless comp at 80 BPM with metronome on 2 and 4.</li>
  <li>Transpose chart to C and G for singer-friendly keys.</li>
</ol>

<h2 id="common-form-pitfalls-in-jam-sessions">Common Form Pitfalls in Jam Sessions</h2>

<p>Jam sessions frequently lose the form at bar 8's Dm7 pivot toward C major or at the bridge entry in Eb. Count aloud through the first chorus without playing — speak key centers ("Ab," "Db," "C," "Eb") at each modulation. Pianists who comp confidently through the A section still stumble when the bridge arrives because they memorized chord names without tonal function.</p>

<p>When calling the tune, announce the key and form length (32 bars AABA). Confirm whether the group uses the common Real Book ending or a tag on the final two bars. One agreed turnaround prevents four musicians from resolving differently.</p>

<h2 id="reading-lead-sheet-annotations">Reading Lead Sheet Annotations</h2>

<p>Real Book charts sometimes abbreviate repeated sections with "DS" or repeat signs that differ from recorded arrangements. Before comping with a play-along, compare your written form to the track's intro length and outro tag. All the Things You Are rarely needs a fancy intro — four bars of Ebmaj7 or Abmaj7 vamp establishes tempo for vocalists.</p>

<p>Mark every secondary dominant with a small arrow pointing to its target tonic. A7 points to Dm; E7 points to Am. Visual arrows speed reading during performance more than rewriting chord symbols.</p>

<h2 id="long-term-memorization-strategy">Long-Term Memorization Strategy</h2>

<p>Memorize in four-bar phrases, not 32 bars at once. Week one: bars 1–8 only, roots and shells daily. Week two: bars 9–16. Week three: bridge. Week four: connect with metronome. Pianists who attempt full-form memorization in one weekend rely on finger memory that collapses under stage adrenaline.</p>

<p>Return to this roadmap whenever a jam session calls the tune in an unfamiliar key.</p>

<h2 id="summary">Summary</h2>

<ul>
  <li>All the Things You Are modulates through Ab, Db, C, G, and Eb centers.</li>
  <li>Each section is linked by ii–V–I progressions worth isolating.</li>
  <li>Guide-tone voice leading connects changes smoothly.</li>
  <li>Rootless Type A/B voicings support combo comping in Ab.</li>
  <li>Map key centers before attempting full-tempo performance.</li>
</ul>
