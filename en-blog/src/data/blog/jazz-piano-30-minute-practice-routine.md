<p>A reliable <strong>jazz piano practice routine</strong> beats random repertoire browsing. Thirty minutes daily, structured in blocks, builds voicings, time feel, ears, and one standard at a time. This plan is for beginners who already know basic seventh chords and can play a slow ii-V-I in C and F.</p>

<p>Use a timer. When a block ends, move on—even if unfinished. Consistency matters more than perfect completion.</p>

<h2 id="overview-six-blocks-of-five-minutes">Overview: Six Blocks of Five Minutes</h2>

<ol>
  <li>Warm-up and rhythm;</li>
  <li>Voicing / harmony;</li>
  <li>Repertoire;</li>
  <li>Ear training;</li>
  <li>Improvisation;</li>
  <li>Review and record.</li>
</ol>

<p>Adjust emphasis by day—Monday heavier on voicings, Wednesday heavier on ear training—but keep the six-block shape.</p>

<h2 id="block-1-warm-up-and-rhythm-0-00-5-00">Block 1: Warm-Up and Rhythm (0:00–5:00)</h2>

<p>Minutes 0–2: Metronome on beats 2 and 4, quarter note = 70. Single-note swing eighths on middle C, then on fifth of current key.</p>

<p>Minutes 2–5: Charleston rhythm on one chord quality—today F7, tomorrow Dm7. Left hand root on 1, right hand shell on 1 and and-of-2. See <a href="https://en.jazzify.jp/blog/jazz-piano-rhythm-practice/">jazz piano rhythm practice</a> for placement detail.</p>

<h2 id="block-2-voicing-harmony-5-00-10-00">Block 2: Voicing / Harmony (5:00–10:00)</h2>

<p>Rotate daily:</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Monday:</strong> Shell voicings ii-V-I in C, F, B-flat (<a href="https://en.jazzify.jp/blog/jazz-piano-shell-voicings/">shell voicings guide</a>);</li>
  <li><strong>Tuesday:</strong> Guide-tone 3rd and 7th only, same keys;</li>
  <li><strong>Wednesday:</strong> Left-hand bass roots + shells (<a href="https://en.jazzify.jp/blog/jazz-piano-left-hand-bass/">left-hand bass</a>);</li>
  <li><strong>Thursday:</strong> One comping pattern through ii-V-I (<a href="https://en.jazzify.jp/blog/jazz-piano-comping-patterns/">comping patterns</a>);</li>
  <li><strong>Friday:</strong> Blues in F shells, bars 1–12 once.</li>
</ul>

<p>Five minutes is short—one key only on slow days, two keys when fresh.</p>

<h2 id="block-3-repertoire-10-00-15-00">Block 3: Repertoire (10:00–15:00)</h2>

<p>Work one tune for a month. Today’s options from our standards list:</p>

<ul>
  <li>Week A: Autumn Leaves—A section roots only;</li>
  <li>Week B: Blue Bossa—bossa rhythm on Cm9;</li>
  <li>Week C: Fly Me to the Moon—turnaround bars only.</li>
</ul>

<p>Do not switch tunes mid-week unless gig deadline forces it. Depth beats novelty.</p>

<h2 id="block-4-ear-training-15-00-20-00">Block 4: Ear Training (15:00–20:00)</h2>

<p>Alternate:</p>

<ul>
  <li>Odd days: sing guide tones on ii-V-I without piano, then check;</li>
  <li>Even days: transcribe two bars of piano comping from a recording (<a href="https://en.jazzify.jp/blog/how-to-transcribe-jazz-piano/">transcription guide</a>).</li>
</ul>

<p>Ear block is non-skippable—five minutes protects long-term growth.</p>

<h2 id="block-5-improvisation-20-00-25-00">Block 5: Improvisation (20:00–25:00)</h2>

<p>Chord-tone solo only for beginners. One chorus on:</p>

<ul>
  <li>Monday: ii-V-I in C;</li>
  <li>Tuesday: F blues;</li>
  <li>Wednesday: current repertoire tune’s A section;</li>
  <li>Thursday: ii-V-I in F;</li>
  <li>Friday: free choice but chord tones on beats 1 and 3.</li>
</ul>

<p>Reference <a href="https://en.jazzify.jp/blog/jazz-piano-improvisation-chord-tones/">chord-tone improvisation</a> when lines wander.</p>

<h2 id="block-6-review-and-record-25-00-30-00">Block 6: Review and Record (25:00–30:00)</h2>

<p>Record 30–60 seconds of today’s best moment—one ii-V-I, four bars of tune, or half chorus blues. Listen once. Note one improvement for tomorrow. Stop practicing after thirty minutes to avoid fatigue undoing good habits.</p>

<h2 id="weekly-rotation-for-variety">Weekly Rotation for Variety</h2>

<p>Keep the six-block frame. Swap inner content:</p>

<ul>
  <li>Week 1: emphasis repertoire + shells;</li>
  <li>Week 2: emphasis ear + chord-tone solo;</li>
  <li>Week 3: add metronome 2-and-4 drills from <a href="https://en.jazzify.jp/blog/jazz-piano-metronome-two-and-four/">metronome on two and four</a>;</li>
  <li>Week 4: performance run—play full form of current tune without stopping.</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="when-you-have-forty-five-minutes">When You Have Forty-Five Minutes</h2>

<p>Add fifteen minutes to repertoire and improvisation only—do not extend social media or random scale books. Extra time means a second chorus of solo or full form with comping, not unfocused noodling.</p>

<h2 id="tracking-progress">Tracking Progress</h2>

<p>Keep a simple log: date, tune, tempo, one sentence (“Db7 tritone sub shaky”). Adults learning jazz benefit from visible streaks—seven days of thirty minutes beats three hours on Sunday.</p>

<h2 id="sample-tuesday-session-detailed">Sample Tuesday Session (Detailed)</h2>

<p>0:00–5:00 — Metronome 2/4 at 36 (effective 72), swing eighths on F mixolydian fragment. 5:00–10:00 — Guide-tone voicings Gm7-C7-Fmaj7, two keys. 10:00–15:00 — Blue Bossa A section, bossa bass on 1 and and-of-2. 15:00–20:00 — Sing Dm7b5-G7 guide tones without piano, check on keyboard. 20:00–25:00 — Chord-tone solo on Blue Bossa A section, one chorus. 25:00–30:00 — Record last eight bars; note rushed and-of-2.</p>

<h2 id="what-to-skip-when-time-is-tight">What to Skip When Time Is Tight</h2>

<p>Never skip warm-up rhythm or repertoire entirely—shorten improvisation to three minutes instead. Voicing block can shrink to one key on busy days. Ear training is the last block to cut; even three minutes of guide-tone singing preserves weekly continuity.</p>

<h2 id="monthly-review-day">Monthly Review Day</h2>

<p>On the last day of the month, play current tune from memory without metronome, then with metronome 2/4. Compare tempos. Update log with new goal tempo for next month (+4 clicks if clean, same if not).</p>

<h2 id="adapting-the-routine-for-adults-with-irregular-schedules">Adapting the Routine for Adults With Irregular Schedules</h2>

<p>Miss a day: do not double next day—resume the six-block plan. Travel days: ear-training block only (sing guide tones on ii-V-I in the hotel). Jet lag days: rhythm block plus review only. The structure survives life better than a rigid ninety-minute regimen abandoned after two weeks.</p>

<h2 id="pairing-routine-with-jazzify-sessions">Pairing Routine With Jazzify Sessions</h2>

<p>Use blocks 2 and 5 for app-guided voicing and solo drills that score timing—human routine supplies repertoire and ear training apps rarely replace. Ten minutes in-app plus twenty minutes with the routine beats thirty random app levels.</p>

<h2 id="weekend-extension-session-optional-45-minutes">Weekend Extension Session (Optional 45 Minutes)</h2>

<p>Add fifteen minutes only to repertoire: run current tune three times—shells, comping, solo—without stopping between choruses. Treat as monthly performance test. Weekday thirty-minute blocks stay unchanged; weekend extension is bonus, not makeup for skipped weekdays.</p>

<h2 id="beginner-milestones-by-month">Beginner Milestones by Month</h2>

<p>Month 1: ii-V-I shells in C, F, B-flat at 72. Month 2: blues form plus one standard A section. Month 3: chord-tone solo on blues. Month 4: full standard with intro/ending at performance tempo minus ten clicks. Adjust if employed weeks slip—repeat a month rather than skipping layers.</p>

<h2 id="protecting-the-habit">Protecting the Habit</h2>

<p>Set a calendar reminder at your fixed practice time with the six-block checklist in the note field. Habit beats motivation on tired days. Thirty minutes completed beats ninety minutes planned and skipped—the routine exists to make starting automatic, not heroic.</p>

<h2 id="sharing-progress-with-a-teacher">Sharing Progress With a Teacher</h2>

<p>Send your weekly log and one recording on review day. Teachers correct faster when they see block allocation—not just hearing a solo without context. Even monthly check-ins keep the routine aligned with long-term goals.</p>

<p>If you study alone, post the same log snippet to a practice partner or community thread—social accountability substitutes for weekly lessons when budget is tight.</p>

<p>Protect the start time, not the mood: sit down even when uninspired; the six blocks usually generate focus within five minutes.</p>

<h2 id="summary">Summary</h2>

<ul>
  <li>Six five-minute blocks: rhythm, voicings, tune, ear, solo, review.</li>
  <li>One standard per month; rotate voicing focus daily.</li>
  <li>Ear training and recording are mandatory blocks.</li>
  <li>Chord-tone improvisation until lines are stable.</li>
  <li>Stop at thirty minutes to protect consistency.</li>
</ul>
